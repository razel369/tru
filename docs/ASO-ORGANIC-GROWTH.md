# PawPair ASO: organic search growth plan

Research date: 2026-07-21

## Executive decision

PawPair should not launch by fighting for `pet care` alone. That query is dominated by brands such as Rover, PetDesk, Chewy, and other apps with hundreds of thousands of US ratings. The launch wedge is a portfolio of specific, high-intent searches where the current results are much weaker:

- `pet medication reminder`
- `dog medication reminder`
- `pet health tracker`
- `dog health tracker`
- `vet records`
- `pet care planner`
- `pet diary`
- `vaccine tracker`
- `puppy tracker`
- `cat medication reminder`

The strategy is to win several narrow searches, accumulate downloads, retention, and ratings, and only then expand into broad terms such as `pet care`, `dog care`, and `cat care`.

ASO cannot guarantee hundreds of thousands of users. Apple says ranking also depends on customer behavior, downloads, and the quantity and quality of ratings and reviews, not only keywords. The realistic objective is hundreds of thousands of qualified search impressions, followed by a conversion system strong enough to turn them into installs.

## What the live market check found

The checks used Apple's public US and Israel storefront search results. Rating counts are storefront-specific snapshots, not estimates of search volume.

### United States

- `pet care` is a poor launch battlefield. Rover, PetDesk, and Chewy appear with roughly 489K to over 1M ratings.
- `pet medication reminder` is unusually open. Several of the leading exact-match apps showed zero or single-digit ratings.
- `dog medication reminder` has the same gap: exact-match competitors are present, but most have almost no rating authority.
- `dog health tracker` is more competitive, but still contains several apps with zero to single-digit ratings alongside a few established products.
- `pet planner` and `pet diary` are weakly defended. They may have lower volume, but they are useful ranking and conversion terms.
- `vet records` has clear intent but stronger incumbents, especially VitusVet. It belongs in the second wave rather than the first launch promise.
- `puppy tracker` is a useful expansion cluster if onboarding and screenshots visibly support puppy routines and milestones.

The most direct metadata competitor is `Pet Care Tracker Dog Cat Log`. Its name and subtitle are aggressively keyword-led, it has strong ratings in the US, and it supports many languages. PawPair must beat it on emotional identity, visual quality, personalization, and conversion rather than copying its generic utility presentation.

### Israel

- Hebrew searches such as `תזכורת תרופות לכלב` returned no relevant result in the live check.
- `בריאות הכלב`, `טיפול בכלב`, and `טיפול בחתול` produced very few relevant care apps and substantial noise from games or unrelated products.
- English searches are more useful in the Israeli storefront. `pet medication reminder`, `dog health tracker`, and `vet records` returned relevant products, but most showed little local rating authority.

Conclusion: Hebrew is a low-competition localization opportunity, but not the market that can supply global scale. English should be the primary growth engine; Hebrew should launch as a fully localized trust and conversion advantage.

## Default screenshot system

Apple can show the first one to three screenshots directly in search results when there is no preview video. Those frames are the main conversion surface.

1. `Everything your pet needs today`
2. `Care that never gets forgotten`
3. `A health history ready for the vet`
4. `Personalized for their breed and age`
5. `Medication reminders without stress`
6. `Meals, walks, grooming, and routines`
7. `Every pet, one beautiful home`
8. `Share care with the people you trust`
9. `Private by design`
10. `Meet the care companion that feels alive`

Creative rules:

- Keep the pet completely unobstructed in the visual center.
- Show the real interface, not concept-only marketing art.
- Use one promise per frame and keep body copy out of the first three frames.
- Match the animal and care task to the query-specific page.
- Show a large, emotional pet first and utility second; this is PawPair's defensible visual advantage.
- Do not lead with Premium, pricing, or a feature checklist.
- Test icon and screenshot changes independently so the cause of a conversion change is measurable.

## Ratings engine

Ratings affect both ranking and conversion, so the prompt must be triggered by earned satisfaction rather than a timer.

Trigger the native rating prompt after one of these moments:

- The user completes care tasks on three separate days.
- The user successfully completes a medication plan several times.
- The user exports or views a useful vet summary.
- The user reaches a visible pet-care streak or milestone.

Never show it immediately after onboarding, after an error, after a missed task, or directly after the paywall. Route unhappy users to private feedback instead of asking them to rate. Apple limits how often the system prompt can be displayed, so each trigger must be intentional.

## Localization order

1. English US: primary launch and research market.
2. English UK, Canada, and Australia: adapt spelling and screenshot language where needed.
3. Hebrew: low-competition, complete RTL product and metadata advantage.
4. Spanish US and Mexico: localize product, screenshots, and keywords together.
5. German and French: add only after English retention and store conversion are healthy.

Localization is not just translation. Every market needs its own title/subtitle decisions, keyword field, first three screenshots, and review baseline. Apple makes localized metadata searchable in relevant storefronts, so this creates additional ranking inventory without duplicating apps.

## Category decision

**Primary:** `Lifestyle`

**Secondary:** `Health & Fitness`

PawPair's core experience is general pet care and a living companion, not medical diagnosis. `Lifestyle` therefore describes the full product more accurately, while `Health & Fitness` supports the tracking side. Reconsider `Medical` only if the shipped product becomes predominantly clinical and its claims, privacy, and review posture support that positioning. Apple requires categories to match the core experience.

## Zero-paid-distribution loop

"No paid ads" must not become "no distribution." Keyword metadata alone will not create the downloads and ratings that Apple's ranking system also uses.

Build these product-led loops:

- A beautiful, private vet summary that users can export and share.
- Shared household care invitations with a clean recipient landing flow.
- A weekly pet-care recap designed to be saved or shared without exposing medical details.
- Breed and life-stage care templates that create useful long-tail landing content.
- A post-onboarding handoff flow for partners, family members, sitters, and walkers.

These loops produce non-paid installs and behavior signals that make ASO stronger. They should not be counted as paid acquisition.

## Funnel model

These are planning scenarios, not industry benchmarks or promises.

| Scenario | Search impressions/month | Store conversion | Installs | Paid conversion | New payers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early traction | 50,000 | 18% | 9,000 | 5% | 450 |
| Target | 250,000 | 24% | 60,000 | 8% | 4,800 |
| Breakout | 500,000 | 28% | 140,000 | 10% | 14,000 |

At a 20% store conversion rate, 100,000 installs require roughly 500,000 qualified impressions. Therefore the first milestone should be 100,000 search impressions per month with healthy retention, not a vanity target of 100,000 installs before ranking authority exists.

## Weekly dashboard

Measure by territory and by App Store source:

- Unique App Store search impressions
- Search product page views
- Search conversion rate
- First-time downloads from search
- Keyword rank for every Tier 1 and Tier 2 term
- Conversion by default page and each custom product page
- Rating count, rating average, and weekly rating velocity
- Day 1, Day 7, and Day 30 retention by acquisition source
- Completed care moments per new user
- Trial starts, paid conversion, refunds, and retention by acquisition source

Use App Store Connect as the source of truth for impressions, search downloads, conversion, and retention. RevenueCat should measure the subscription funnel, but it cannot replace App Store search attribution.

## 90-day execution plan

### Days 0-14: build the indexed foundation

- Ship the recommended English metadata.
- Ship complete Hebrew localization.
- Produce the default screenshot set and three custom product pages.
- Implement deep links for medication, health records, and personalized onboarding.
- Instrument care completion, review eligibility, and subscription events.
- Add the satisfaction-based rating prompt.

### Days 15-30: earn the first ranking signals

- Focus onboarding on the first completed care task in under two minutes.
- Collect private feedback before aggressively requesting ratings.
- Track every Tier 1 term weekly.
- Fix crashes, reminder failures, and broken pet data before changing keywords; poor behavior signals will cancel out metadata gains.
- Start one Product Page Optimization test, changing screenshots only.

### Days 31-60: compound the winning intent

- Keep keywords that are moving toward the top 20 and produce retained users.
- Replace terms with no visibility after a full version cycle.
- Apply the winning screenshot treatment only when the result has meaningful confidence.
- Add English UK/Canada/Australia localization.
- Launch the shareable vet summary and household invitation loop.

### Days 61-90: expand, do not dilute

- Add Spanish localization if English activation and retention are stable.
- Create life-stage pages for puppies, kittens, and senior pets only when the in-app experience supports each promise.
- Move one broad term into a more prominent field only after several narrow terms rank well.
- Review search cohorts against paid conversion. Keep terms that create valuable, retained users, not merely downloads.

## Decision gates

- Do not scale localization if Day 7 retention is weak; fix the product first.
- Do not replace metadata after a few days; compare full version cycles and territory-level data.
- Do not chase a high-volume term if its search page converts poorly or attracts the wrong users.
- Do not claim medical outcomes or imply veterinary replacement.
- Do not buy ratings or gate features behind a positive review.
- Do not judge ASO by rank alone. The winning term produces impressions, installs, retention, and revenue.
